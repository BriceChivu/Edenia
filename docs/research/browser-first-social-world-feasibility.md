# Browser-first social-world feasibility and future packaging paths

Date: 2026-08-30

## Answer

An adult-first, desktop-browser social learning world is technically credible and is the lowest-risk first platform for a solo developer. A 2D top-down or isometric world, keyboard/pointer movement, a normal YouTube player, room-scoped text chat, presence, and a solo fallback all fit current browser capabilities. Nothing in that choice prevents a later installable PWA, desktop wrapper, Steam build, or mobile wrapper.

The viable browser shape is a **hybrid web application**: render the visual world in Canvas/WebGL, while keeping the YouTube player, chat, menus, forms, and accessible alternatives as ordinary DOM. Use authenticated, room-scoped realtime channels and keep platform-specific features behind a boundary that a future wrapper can replace.

There is one immediate product-level blocker: **Edenia cannot award points, cosmetics, or other benefits because a learner watched a YouTube video.** YouTube's current policy prohibits API clients from offering rewards for engaging with YouTube, directly or indirectly, including viewing content. The proposed “watch for 30 minutes, then spend the points on a hat” loop is therefore not a viable YouTube-based mechanic as stated. This applies regardless of whether playback is measured automatically or confirmed manually. The social-world exploration can proceed, but its reward decision must be decoupled from YouTube viewing before a prototype represents a credible product direction. This is a policy reading, not legal advice.

## Confirmed constraints

### YouTube is a separate, unobscured surface—not a texture inside the world

- YouTube explicitly forbids incentives, rewards, or compensation for engaging with YouTube Applications directly or indirectly, including by viewing content. Cosmetic currency and unlocks are rewards even when they have no cash value. [YouTube Developer Policies, Playback Integrity](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies#playback_integrity)
- An embedded player must be at least 200 by 200 pixels; YouTube recommends at least 480 by 270 for a 16:9 player with controls. Edenia must not cover any part of it with overlays, frames, speech bubbles, custom controls, or world effects, and must not alter undocumented player behavior. A decorative laptop or room can surround the player rectangle, but nothing should cross over it. [YouTube Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality#youtube_embedded_player_and_video_playback)
- Autoplay may start only when the player is visible and more than half visible, with at most one autoplaying player per screen. YouTube also prohibits enabling background play when the app is minimized or closed. A learner-initiated, foreground-only player is the safe baseline. [YouTube Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality#autoplay_and_scripted_playbacks), [YouTube policy guide](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies-guide#your-api-service-must-reflect-a-users-standard-experience-on-youtube)
- The IFrame API exposes playback state and elapsed playback time, not attention, comprehension, or learning. Those signals can support player controls or resuming a session, subject to policy, but they cannot prove that the learner studied. [YouTube IFrame API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference#Playback_status)
- Loading an embedded player shares some data with YouTube; playback shares more. YouTube recommends disabling autoplay to limit pre-interaction sharing. API clients must also handle Made for Kids videos specially: look up that status, turn off tracking for those players, and comply with applicable law. Adult-only enrollment does not guarantee that selected videos are not designated Made for Kids. [YouTube Developer Policies, data handling](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies#handling_youtube_data_and_content)
- Browser embeds identify the API client through the HTTP `Referer`. Packaged desktop and mobile clients need an approved app identity in `Referer`/`origin`, and YouTube says embedded-player WebViews should use an OS-provided WebView where available. Any wrapper must prove this exact seam before it becomes a distribution commitment. [YouTube Required Minimum Functionality, client identity and WebViews](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality#api_client_identity_and_credentials)

**Inference:** the safest visual composition is a normal DOM iframe placed in a study panel beside or above the world canvas. A cross-origin YouTube iframe cannot be read or drawn into Edenia's canvas under the browser's same-origin boundary, and YouTube policy independently rules out masking or compositing over the player. [MDN same-origin policy](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Defenses/Same-origin_policy)

The current Edenia scoring mechanism deserves a separate policy audit. This note does not change or adjudicate the existing product; it records the constraint that the new social-world design cannot carry “points for YouTube viewing” forward unchanged.

### Browser rendering and input are sufficient for the proposed 2D scope

- Canvas 2D and WebGL are widely available; WebGL can render accelerated 2D or 3D scenes without a plugin, though actual device capabilities and context loss still require feature detection and recovery. WebGPU remains non-Baseline because some widely used browsers do not support it. It should be an optional future acceleration path, not the minimum platform for this product. [MDN WebGL](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API), [MDN WebGPU](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGPU_API)
- Pointer Events provide one model for mouse, pen, and touch, while the Gamepad API is broadly available with some support variation. Keyboard and pointer should therefore be the required controls; touch and gamepad can be additive mappings rather than separate world logic. [MDN Pointer Events](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Pointer_events/Using_Pointer_Events), [MDN Gamepad API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Gamepad_API)
- Canvas pixels do not provide semantic HTML to assistive technology. Chat, the YouTube player, inventory, settings, navigation shortcuts, and a non-spatial route to the learning activity must remain in the DOM even if the room itself is visual. [MDN canvas accessibility](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/canvas#accessibility)
- Browsers usually stop `requestAnimationFrame` and throttle timers in hidden tabs. Page visibility can be observed, but the app cannot promise continuous simulation or exact “online now” presence while backgrounded. [MDN Page Visibility API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Page_Visibility_API#policies_in_place_to_aid_background_page_performance)
- Web Audio generally requires a user gesture before sound starts. Ambient music and effects need explicit user activation and persistent mute/volume controls. [MDN Web Audio best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices#autoplay_policy)

**Inference:** a top-down or isometric 2D scene is comfortably inside the browser envelope; visual-production complexity, not browser rendering capability, is likely to be the binding constraint. The rendering-library choice should follow a small visual/performance comparison rather than drive the art direction.

### Supabase Realtime can validate the room model, but movement must be budgeted

Supabase already provides the right categories: Presence for slow-changing state such as who is in a room, and Broadcast for low-latency chat, cursor, position, or game events. Presence is explicitly not intended for high-frequency movement. [Supabase Realtime](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime), [Supabase Presence](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/presence)

As checked on 2026-08-30, the hosted Free plan allows 200 concurrent connections, 100 messages per second, 20 presence messages per second, 100 channels per connection, and five presence calls per client per 30 seconds. A delivered event counts as well as a sent event, so fan-out multiplies movement traffic: a broadcast to 100 subscribers counts as 100 events. These are current plan limits, not permanent product guarantees. [Supabase Realtime limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits), [Supabase Realtime settings](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/settings#max-events-per-second)

Consequences:

- Early validation with small study rooms is feasible on the existing service, but a naive per-frame or per-pointer position broadcast is not. Send only meaningful movement changes at a deliberately low rate and interpolate locally; reserve Presence for join/leave and coarse status.
- “Public study room” should describe discoverability, not transport security. Realtime public channels allow anyone with the project's anonymous key to subscribe and send. Authenticated rooms should use private channels with RLS policies tied to room membership. [Supabase Realtime Authorization](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/authorization)
- Reconnects, dropped messages, rate-limit closures, and stale presence are normal distributed-system conditions. The solo experience cannot depend on a perfect connection, and remote avatar position is presentation state rather than durable learner progress.
- Transient Broadcast is not, by itself, a moderation record. If chat must be reportable or reviewable, its authoritative retention and deletion rules need a separate decision rather than being inferred from realtime replay.

### Installability and packaged distribution remain available, with different costs

| Route | Confirmed capability | Decision consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browser/PWA | A Web App Manifest can declare name, icons, start URL, orientation, and display mode. Installation support and prompting differ by browser; iOS uses a manual Add to Home Screen flow. A PWA does not require Edenia to ship an executable. [W3C Web App Manifest](https://www.w3.org/TR/appmanifest/), [web.dev installation guide](https://web.dev/learn/pwa/installation) | This is the cheapest first installable form, but it is not equivalent to App Store or Steam distribution. Offline world assets are possible; YouTube playback and realtime social presence remain network services. |
| Tauri 2 | Tauri uses OS webviews—WebView2 on Windows and WebKit/WKWebView on Apple platforms—supports desktop, Android, and iOS targets, and produces platform installers/store builds. Webview versions vary with the device, and development adds Rust plus platform toolchains and signing. [Tauri architecture](https://v2.tauri.app/concept/architecture/), [webview versions](https://v2.tauri.app/reference/webview-versions/), [distribution](https://v2.tauri.app/distribute/) | Promising for a later thin wrapper, especially because its OS-webview model aligns with YouTube's stated WebView preference. It still needs a real YouTube identity/referrer, auth, rendering, and input spike before selection. |
| Capacitor | Capacitor can wrap an existing HTML/CSS/JavaScript app for iOS and Android and expose native SDKs through plugins. [Capacitor documentation](https://capacitorjs.com/docs) | A credible mobile-specific wrapper if mobile becomes important; it still creates and maintains native projects and store releases. There is no need to choose between it and Tauri during browser validation. |
| Electron | Electron embeds Chromium and Node.js to ship one JavaScript codebase on Windows, macOS, and Linux; distribution adds executables, signing, and updates. Native integrations require platform/architecture builds, and remote content must never receive Node integration. [Electron introduction](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/), [distribution](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/distribution-overview), [security](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/security) | It offers consistent Chromium behavior and mature desktop packaging, but its bundled renderer is not the OS-provided WebView named by YouTube. Do not assume YouTube compliance for this route without explicit validation. |
| Steam | Steam requires an uploaded executable/build with launch options and depots. Steamworks client API integration is optional, although the SDK tools are used to upload. Steam Direct currently charges US$100 per product, requires a 30-day wait, and requires a public Coming Soon page for at least two weeks. [SteamPipe upload](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/uploading), [Steamworks API overview](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/api), [Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect/) | A browser URL alone is not a Steam product; Edenia would need a desktop wrapper. Steam identity, achievements, friends, and overlay should be optional adapters, not foundations of the browser account or social model. Valve warns that its overlay does not work with ordinary browser rendering without a difficult native/offscreen composition path. [Steam Overlay](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/overlay) |

Native mobile-store packaging also turns chat operations into a distribution requirement. Apple requires UGC/social apps to filter objectionable material, support reporting with timely responses, block abusive users, and publish contact information. Google Play likewise requires robust ongoing moderation plus in-app reporting and blocking for publicly accessible UGC. Adult-only scope reduces minor-specific obligations, but it does not remove these store requirements. [Apple App Review Guideline 1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#user-generated-content), [Google Play UGC policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937)

## Choices that preserve later paths

The following are recommendations inferred from the confirmed constraints; they are not implementation decisions for this ticket.

1. **Make the web runtime canonical.** The browser should contain the complete solo and social learning experience. A desktop/mobile package should initially be a thin host, not a fork of product logic.
2. **Keep a DOM/canvas split.** Render world tiles, characters, and movement in Canvas/WebGL. Keep YouTube, chat, forms, inventory, moderation actions, and accessible navigation in DOM layers that never cover the player.
3. **Target the common baseline.** Require WebGL or Canvas, keyboard, and pointer input. Feature-detect optional gamepad, touch refinements, worker rendering, and WebGPU. Do not make WebGPU or native APIs part of the world-state model.
4. **Describe input as actions.** “Move,” “interact,” “sit,” and “open study” should be independent of WASD, arrows, touch targets, or a controller. That preserves mobile and Steam-controller paths without designing either now.
5. **Keep durable state platform-neutral.** Learner identity and progress should remain server/browser-domain concepts, not Steam IDs, device paths, or wrapper APIs. Platform accounts can be linked later. Browser storage, native secure storage, auth callbacks, and mobile deep links need replaceable adapters; Supabase already supports explicit mobile deep-link redirect URIs. [Supabase redirect URLs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/redirect-urls#mobile-deep-linking-uris)
6. **Treat origin and lifecycle as packaging seams.** YouTube identity/referrer, Supabase redirect URLs, session refresh, foreground/background events, notifications, file access, and updates vary by host. Centralize those differences rather than scattering environment checks through the world.
7. **Do not promise Steam features yet.** Shipping a wrapped build is credible; overlay, achievements, Steam friends, ownership checks, and controller certification are separate value decisions. Validate only the features the product later decides it needs.
8. **Accept the rewrite boundary honestly.** Web code can be reused by PWA, Tauri, Capacitor, or Electron. Moving later to a native game engine would reuse art, content, protocols, and domain contracts, but would substantially rewrite the client. Browser-first preserves wrapper paths, not every possible engine path.

## Decision

Proceed with **desktop browser as the validation platform**, without selecting a permanent game framework or native wrapper. The browser is capable enough for the proposed 2D social-learning experience, is aligned with Edenia's current delivery model, and gives a solo developer the cheapest route to test belonging, study return, discovery, and solo resilience.

That decision is conditional on four boundaries:

1. Replace “points for watching YouTube” with a reward model that is not contingent on YouTube engagement; do not disguise the same incentive as a generic session reward.
2. Present YouTube as an unobscured, foreground DOM player and treat its playback signals as player state, not evidence of attention or learning.
3. Validate social rooms with bounded occupancy, private authenticated channels, slow Presence, and budgeted Broadcast rather than continuous networked simulation.
4. Before committing to desktop/Steam or mobile distribution, run a narrow packaging proof for YouTube identity/referrer, Supabase auth return, WebGL/Canvas behavior, keyboard/pointer/controller focus, lifecycle, signing, and any truly required store feature.

PWA installability may be added after the browser experience is useful. Tauri, Capacitor, Electron, and Steam remain credible future investigations, not current commitments.
